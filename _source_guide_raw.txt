. Chapter One
Character Creation
In this chapter we will go over the steps that players will follow when creating their
character. This chapter will also cover the basic information players will need to
understand when making choices for their character(s). Players are free to follow
this process in any order they choose so long as they complete each step before
playing the character in a campaign.
. Section 1: character Sheet
The character Sheet is where players will write down and keep track of all their
characters information. Players may choose to use any piece of paper or notebook
they may have available in place of a standard PC character Sheet so long as it
contains all the required information. (Note: a PDF ﬁle of the standard Psion
Chronicles character Sheet is available for download and print on our website,
there will also be a link and QR code at the back of this book.)
The front page of the character Sheet is the most important page. This is where
players will ﬁnd the character's name, and Psionic Background (see section 3 of
this chapter), soul level, experience, body, mind, and soul pools, as well as the
character's attribute scores and modiﬁers. The front page should also contain a
short list of simple combat actions the character can take for quick reference,
along with the characters chakra chart, attribute save modiﬁers, and Defense
Score. (See section 2 of this chapter for information on how to determine attribute
scores and what they mean.)
The second page of the character Sheet is the skills page. THis is where players
will ﬁnd the complete list of skills their character can perform along with spots to
mark whether or not they are proﬁcient with each skill. The list also shows which
attribute modiﬁer applies to each skill, and a small section next to each skill to
write and track the modiﬁer they add for each skill. (See section 4 of this chapter
for a complete list of skills and an explanation on how they work.)
The next few pages are the core of character play in Psion Chronicles. This is
where players will write down and keep track of the Kinetic Techniques the

----- PAGE 2 -----
character knows and uses (see chapter two full a complete list and breakdown of
the Kinetic Techniques and how to use them during gameplay). When ﬁlling out this
list players should include the technique name, which Kinetic it belongs to, and the
details of the technique's effects and rules.
Following the KT list the next page of the character Sheet is the character's
inventory. Here players will keep track of their carry weight, and a list of all the
items their character has equipped and on the person. The remaining pages of the
character Sheet are meant for any personal notes the players see ﬁt to write down
about their character, this may include a description of their appearance, a short
backstory for their character, or even a journal cataloging the events of the
campaign.
The character Sheet is the most important tool a player needs when playing Psion
Chronicles.
. Section 2: Attributes
The Psion Chronicles uses a six attribute system for player characters, NPCs, and
Monsters. These attributes are Strength (STR), Agility (AGI), Constitution (CON),
Intelligence (INT), Wisdom (WIS), and Charisma (CHA). These six attributes are
decided between body and mind with STR, AGI, and CON being the three body
attributes and INT, WIS, and CHA being the three mind attributes. The total value of
a characters Body pool is the total value of the three body attribute scores when
added together, the total value of a characters mind pool is the total value of the
three mind attribute scores when added together.
For example a character with 15 STR 12 AGI and 13 CON would have a total body
pool of 40. A character with 22 INT 18 WIS and 16 CHA would have a total mind
pool of 56.
To determine a character's attribute score players roll 8D12 and remove the two
lowest values, then add ten to each of the remaining values and assign each value
to an attribute.

Now that we understand attribute scores and their modiﬁers let's go over other key
functions of speciﬁc attributes.
Strength (STR)
Strength is a characters raw physical power. This attribute determines a characters
unarmed damage, climbing speed, and jump distance. The base climbing speed for
all characters is 15ft, to determine a character's individual climbing speed add or
subtract 5ft for each modiﬁer point in STR (for example: a character with a 25 STR
has a +3 modiﬁer, meaning that we add 5ft times 3 to their climbing speed giving a
total of 15+15=30 climbing speed. Jump distance works the same way as climbing
speed with the base being 15ft then adding or subtracting 5ft per modiﬁer point.
Agility (AGI)
Agility is a characters speed and reﬂexes, as well as their ﬁner motor skills. This
attribute determines a character's movement speed, initiative roll, and their
unarmored defense score (in addition to Constitution). AGI modiﬁes a character's
movement speed in the same way that STR modiﬁes it a characters jump distance
and climbing speed, with the bass being 30 ft and either adding or subtracting 5 ft
each modiﬁer. When rolling for initiative, add your AGI modiﬁer to the dice roll. A
character's unarmored defense score (DS) is 10 10 plus AGI modiﬁer + CON
modiﬁer.
Constitution (CON)
Constitution is a character's durability, the measure of how much they can
withstand and how long they can exert themselves. This attribute determines a
character's swimming speed, carry weight, and their defense score (in addition to
Agility). Swimming speed is determined in the same method as climbing speed,
jump distance, and movement speed, with the base being 15 then adding or
subtracting 5ft for each CON modiﬁer point. A character's carry weight is the
amount of weight they can hold in their inventory, the base is 100 lb adding or
subtracting 10 lb per modiﬁer point.
Intelligence (INT)

Intelligence is the measure of a characters logic abilities. This attribute determines
their crafting and repair time.
Wisdom (WIS)
Wisdom is a character's understanding of the world around them.
Charisma (CHA)
Charisma is a character's ability to charm other characters and creatures.
. Section 3: Psionic Backgrounds
A character's Psionic Background is who they were in the old world and serves as a
launching point for the type of character a player wishes to roleplay. This book
includes nine Psionic Backgrounds players may choose from, each Background
grants a character three points in one attribute, or two points in two attributes, as
well as a boost to either their body pool, mind pool, or both. Each Background also
grants a character proﬁciency in three skills, two combat proﬁciencies (two
weapon types, or one weapon type and one technique type), and one free beginner
level technique.

Section 4: Skills
When a Player Character (PC) attempts to perform a task in-game the Game
Master (GM) may ask the pler to make a skill check for a speciﬁc skill. A skill check
is made by ﬁrst checking the speciﬁc skill. The GM has asked you to use to see
which attribute modiﬁes that skill and whether or not you have proﬁciency in the
skill. The player then rolls a d20, adds the appropriate modiﬁer, and if they have
proﬁciency in the skill they add their proﬁciency bonus. A PC's proﬁciency bonus is
+3 at level 1 moving up to +4 at level 15 and ﬁnally +5 at level 30.
In addition to the three skill Proﬁciencies granted by a character's Psionic
Background players will select two other skills to be proﬁcient in. The following is a
list of all the skills available to characters in The Psion Chronicles, with a brief
description of how they work, separated by which attribute modiﬁes the skill check.
Strength (STR)
Muscle: Raw physical power used to lift, break, or shove heavy objects.
Laborer's Tools: Skill in using heavy equipment like hammers, saws, and
masonry tools.
Intimidation: Using physical presence or brute force to coerce or scare others.
Athletics: Proﬁciency in climbing, sprinting swimming, and jumping
Grapple: The ability to pin, restrain, or wrestle opponents in close combat.
Force: Applying concentrated power to break through barriers or heavy
obstacles.
Agility (AGI)
Acrobatics: Fluid movement used for balancing, tumbling, and landing safely.
Stealth: Moving silently and staying hidden from the sight of others.

Section 5: Combat Proﬁciencies Weapon Types
There are many weapons available to players and NPCs in The Psion Chronicles,
ranging from swords and bows to ﬁrearms and laser guns, there are also weapons
like wands and staffs that channel the wielder's Ki into ranged attacks. Every
weapon in the world is classiﬁed by it's weapon type, and subtype. A character may
be proﬁcient with a weapon type which includes all weapon subtypes that fall
under that category, or just a weapon subtype. For example, Heavy Weapon is a
weapon type and a subtype of a heavy weapon would be a Warhammer. If a

----- PAGE 16 -----
character has proﬁciency in heavy weapons they have proﬁciency with
warhammers, however, if a character has proﬁciency with warhammers, they do
not necessarily have proﬁciency with heavy weapons. After character creation, the
only way a character can gain proﬁciency in a weapon type is if they gain
proﬁciency in all subtypes that fall under that weapon type.
The following is a list of each weapon type and subtype available in The Psion
Chronicles (for a complete list of available weapons please the Game Master's
guide).
Strength Weapon Types
Heavy Weapons
Heavy weapons are a melee weapon type deﬁned by their immense mass and the
crushing force they generate. Because of their weight and size, these armaments
typically require two hands to wield effectively, sacriﬁcing speed for overwhelming
impact. In this system, Strength is the primary modiﬁer added to all Heavy Weapon
damage rolls, representing the raw power required to swing these massive tools
and drive them through thick defenses.
Heavy Weapon Subtypes
Heavy weapons are categorized by their head shape, weight distribution, and
intended method of destruction:
Great Hammers: Massive, long-handled tools of destruction designed for
maximum kinetic impact. They excel at delivering devastating blunt force trauma
capable of crushing armor and bone alike, making them ideal for shattering the
most resilient foes.
Great Swords: Colossal two-handed blades that bridge the gap between a polearm
and a standard sword. These weapons provide signiﬁcant reach and use their
extreme weight to shear through targets, offering a versatile mix of cutting power
and defensive range.
Great Axes: Top-heavy weapons featuring large, weighted blades. They are
designed to concentrate all the wielder’s power into a single, high-momentum

----- PAGE 17 -----
swinging arc to cleave through thick defenses and deliver catastrophic rending
damage.
Maces: Durable, high-impact bludgeoning weapons built for eﬃciency. While
shorter than their "Great" counterparts, they feature weighted heads speciﬁcally
designed to dent plate armor and transmit bone-breaking force through even the
thickest protective padding.
Axes: Sturdy, single-edged hafted weapons built for precise chopping. These
represent a middle ground in the heavy category, relying on a balanced weight
distribution to deliver deep, clean wounds while remaining slightly more
maneuverable than larger cleaving tools.
Archery
Archery is a ranged weapon type that focuses on launching projjectiles—such as
arrows, small rocks, or metal balls—at distant targets. Whether using the
mechanical tension of a bow or the centrifugal force of a sling, these weapons rely
on the user's physical power to drive the projectile home. In this system, Strength is
the primary modiﬁer added to all Archery damage rolls, representing the force
behind the draw or the speed of the swing.
Archery Subtypes
Archery weapons are categorized by their frame, mechanical operation, and
intended effective range:
Longbows: Tall, powerful bows—often as tall as the archer—designed for long-
distance engagements. They require signiﬁcant effort to draw but offer the
greatest range and piercing power of all handheld bows, making them ideal for
open-ﬁeld combat.
Shortbows: Compact and lightweight bows built for mobility. These are designed
for quick ﬁring in close-quarters skirmishes or for use while mounted on
horseback. While they have a shorter effective range than longbows, they are much
easier to handle in cramped environments or on the move.
Slings: Simple but deadly tools consisting of a ﬂexible strap used to hurl stones or
lead bullets. By whirling the sling in a circular motion and releasing one cord, the

----- PAGE 18 -----
user launches the projectile at high velocities. They are prized for being easy to
conceal and for having readily available ammunition.
Slingshots: Handheld "Y" shaped frames that use elastic tension to ﬁre small
projectiles like pellets or stones. Unlike the traditional sling, the slingshot is braced
by the user’s hand and pulled back directly, offering a more intuitive aiming method
for mid-range hunting or harassment.
Fist Weapons
Fist weapons are specialized gear designed to enhance unarmed combat by
adding weight, hardness, or cutting edges to the wearer’s natural strikes. Worn over
the hands rather than held, these weapons allow a combatant to maintain the
speed and versatility of a brawler while dealing lethal damage. In this system,
Strength is the primary modiﬁer added to all Fist Weapon damage rolls,
representing the raw physical power and muscle behind the impact of the strike.
Fist Weapon Subtypes
Fist weapons are categorized by their coverage, weight distribution, and the type of
force they deliver:
Knuckles: Compact frames of metal or reinforced material that sit across the
ﬁngers. These are the most discreet ﬁst weapons, designed to focus the force of a
punch into a small, hard surface area. They favor speed and blunt force, allowing
the user to break bone without adding signiﬁcant weight or bulk to the arm.
Full Fists: Heavy, reinforced gloves or gauntlets that cover the entire hand and
wrist. By adding signiﬁcant mass to the hands, they turn every swing into a
weighted hammer blow. These provide the highest impact force and often offer
defensive beneﬁts, allowing the wearer to parry incoming attacks with their
reinforced forearms.
Knuckle Blades: Weighted grips or gloves that incorporate perpendicular or
forward-facing blades. These weapons transform the natural "point-and-strike"
motion of a punch into a lethal stabbing or slashing attack. They are designed for
precision, combining the instinct of a brawler with the piercing power of a dagger.
Agility Weapon Types

Light Weapons
Light weapons are small, easily maneuverable tools designed for speed and
precision rather than raw power. Because these weapons rely on hitting vital spots
and ﬁnding gaps in armor, you add your Agility modiﬁer to the damage roll instead
of Strength. Their low weight and ease of use make them the primary choice for
stealthy characters and those who prefer the rapid-ﬁre nature of dual-wielding.
Light Weapon Subtypes
Light weapons are categorized by their length, balance, and intended method of
deployment:
Knives: Small, everyday utility blades that are easily concealed on one's person.
While they have the shortest reach of any blade, they are perfect for surprise
strikes and are often balanced speciﬁcally for throwing at distant targets.
Daggers: Purpose-built combat blades featuring double edges and reinforced
points. These are the gold standard for precision, offering a much higher potential
for lethal critical hits when striking an unaware foe from the shadows.
Batons: Short, blunt instruments such as clubs or weighted rods. These allow an
agile ﬁghter to deliver rapid, bludgeoning strikes to incapacitate or stun opponents
without having to rely on a sharpened edge.
Short Swords: The largest weapons in the light category, these balanced blades
provide a reliable reach in close-quarters combat. They offer a perfect middle
ground between high-speed offense and the ability to parry for defensive utility.
Quick Weapons
Quick weapons are a category of gear designed for speed, precision, and rapid-ﬁre
utility. Unlike heavy armaments that rely on brute strength, these weapons favor
high attack frequency and seamless transitions between offense and defense. In
this system, Agility is the primary modiﬁer added to all Quick Weapon damage rolls,
representing the wielder's ability to strike vital gaps and utilize momentum rather
than raw power.
Quick Weapon Subtypes

----- PAGE 20 -----
Quick weapons are categorized by their mechanical design, ease of concealment,
and the ﬂuid nature of their deployment:
Tonfa: These L-shaped batons are held along the forearm, allowing for ﬂuid
transitions between blocking and striking. They provide excellent defensive utility
while remaining lethal in close quarters, utilizing the length of the arm to generate
swift, swinging force.
Wrist Blades: Mounted directly to bracers or gauntlets, these blades sit on the
outside of the arm. This unique design leaves the wearer’s hands free for grappling,
climbing, or interacting with the environment without having to stow the weapon.
Hand Crossbows: Compact, one-handed ranged weapons built for "pistol-style"
skirmishing. They are ideal for rapid deployment during a move and are easily
concealed compared to their larger counterparts, favoring the mobile marksman.
Blowguns: Simple but effective tubes used to launch small darts. While they lack
heavy impact, their high rate of ﬁre and silent operation make them an ideal tool for
rapid, precise application of various tactical effects from a distance.
Thrown Weapons
Thrown weapons are specialized projectiles designed to be launched by hand
rather than ﬁred from a bow or mechanical device. They rely on aerodynamic
eﬃciency, weight distribution, and the user’s precision to strike targets at range.
Unlike bulky melee weapons, these are crafted for balance and ﬂight, allowing a
combatant to maintain distance while dealing lethal damage. In this system, Agility
is the primary modiﬁer added to all Thrown Weapon damage rolls, representing the
precision, ﬂick-of-the-wrist velocity, and perfect timing required to maximize
aerodynamic force.
Thrown Weapon Subtypes
Thrown weapons are categorized by their ﬂight mechanics, weight distribution, and
intended rate of ﬁre:
Shuriken: Small, ﬂat, and often multi-pointed projectiles. These are the most
discreet thrown weapons, designed for rapid-ﬁre deployment and high-volume

----- PAGE 21 -----
strikes. Their low proﬁle makes them easy to conceal and draw in a split second,
favoring speed and harassment over heavy impact.
Throwing Knives: Speciﬁcally weighted blades balanced from hilt to tip. By
engineering the blade to rotate predictably in the air, these weapons ensure the
point strikes the target with maximum piercing force even from a signiﬁcant
distance. They turn a standard throwing motion into a reliable, lethal strike.
Darts: Aerodynamic, needle-like projectiles often stabilized by small ﬁns or
feathers. These prioritize speed and accuracy over mass, making them ideal for
delivering pinpoint strikes to exposed weak points. They are designed for precision,
allowing the user to bypass heavy defenses through sheer accuracy.
Constitution Weapon Types
Firearms
Firearms represent the pinnacle of high-impact ranged combat, governed by
precision and disciplined breath control rather than raw manual dexterity. Unlike
traditional weapons, these mechanical tools require internal stability to harness
their true lethality. In this system, Constitution (CON) is the primary modiﬁer added
to all Firearm damage rolls, representing the shooter’s physical stamina to absorb
recoil, lung capacity to steady aim, and the grit required to maintain a solid
sightline through the weapon's kick.
Firearm Subtypes
Firearms are categorized by their effective range, recoil management, and the
speciﬁc physical demands they place on the shooter:
Riﬂes: Designed for long-range engagement and high-velocity impact, riﬂes
demand total internal stability. These weapons require the shooter to brace their
entire body against the stock to suppress their heartbeat and hold their breath. A
high Constitution provides the stillness necessary for lethal accuracy, ensuring that
kinetic energy is delivered into the target at great distances.
Handguns: Semi-automatic sidearms that prioritize ﬁre rate and versatility.
Because these weapons are lighter, they produce a violent "muzzle climb" during
rapid ﬁre. A high Constitution represents the wrist and forearm endurance needed

----- PAGE 22 -----
to ﬁght the weapon’s upward jerk, ensuring that every follow-up shot in a sequence
hits with maximum force and consistency.
Revolvers: High-pressure mechanical weapons known for their reliability and
immense stopping power. Without a slide to absorb energy, the raw recoil of a
revolver is transferred directly into the user’s arm. Constitution acts as the physical
threshold to withstand this shockwave, allowing the shooter to ﬁre heavy rounds
that would otherwise cause physical strain or fatigue.
Explosives
Explosives are volatile, single-use or tactical armaments designed to deal
devastating damage over an area. Unlike precision weapons, the effectiveness of
an explosive is tied to the user’s physical fortitude and steady hand under pressure;
therefore, Constitution is the primary modiﬁer added to all Explosive damage rolls,
representing the durability and grit required to handle such unstable payloads.
Explosive Subtypes
Explosives are categorized by their deployment method, trigger mechanisms, and
the tactical role they serve on the battleﬁeld:
Grenades: Compact, thrown projectiles designed for mid-range engagement. These
explosives typically trigger on impact or after a short fuse, making them ideal for
ﬂushing out enemies from cover or clearing small rooms. They offer a versatile
balance of mobility and immediate destructive potential.
Mines: Stationary, pressure-sensitive, or proximity-triggered devices. Mines are
tactical tools used for area denial and ambushes, requiring careful placement to
catch unsuspecting foes. These represent a more calculated approach to
demolition, turning the environment itself into a weapon.
Improvised Explosives: Unconventional, unstable devices cobbled together from
salvaged components or scrap materials. While often unpredictable, these "jury-
rigged" explosives provide high-risk, high-reward utility in the ﬁeld. They are the
weapons of choice for those who favor raw, chaotic power over standard
engineering.
Volatile Weapons

----- PAGE 23 -----
Volatile weapons are high-risk, high-reward tools of destruction that utilize unstable
energy, pressurized fuel, or reactive chemicals. Designed for maximum
devastation, these weapons require immense physical endurance to operate safely.
